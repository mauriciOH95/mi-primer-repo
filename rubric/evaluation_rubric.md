# Evaluation Rubric

## Scoring scale

Use a 1–5 scale for each dimension.

- **5 — Excellent:** No meaningful issue is noticeable.
- **4 — Good:** Minor issue, but overall quality remains natural and usable.
- **3 — Acceptable:** Noticeable issue that affects polish or naturalness, but meaning remains clear.
- **2 — Poor:** Major issue or multiple issues that clearly reduce quality.
- **1 — Failure:** Severe issue that materially harms intelligibility, naturalness, or usability.

## Dimensions

### 1. Pronunciation
Measures whether words, phonemes, stress patterns, names, numbers, abbreviations, and borrowed terms are pronounced correctly for the target Spanish variety.

### 2. Prosody
Measures intonation, sentence stress, emphasis, phrasing, and whether pitch movement matches the intended communicative function.

### 3. Rhythm & pace
Measures speech rate, pause placement, timing, and whether the utterance flows naturally without rushed or dragged segments.

### 4. Voice consistency
Measures whether vocal identity, timbre, pitch range, loudness, and speaking style remain stable throughout the sample.

### 5. Audio quality
Measures synthesis artifacts such as distortion, clipping, metallic texture, glitches, dropouts, or other non-linguistic defects.

### 6. Naturalness
Holistic judgment of whether the utterance sounds plausibly human and appropriate for the intended context.

## Evaluation rule

Evaluators should score each dimension independently and then add failure tags where useful. Avoid lowering several dimensions for the exact same defect unless the defect genuinely affects each one.

## Calibration note

Before scaling the benchmark, manually label an initial calibration set and compare evaluator agreement. Any dimension with low agreement should be rewritten with more concrete examples before expanding the dataset.
